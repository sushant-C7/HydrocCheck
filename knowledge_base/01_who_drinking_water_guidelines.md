# WHO drinking-water quality guidance

## Scope of the guidance
The World Health Organization's Guidelines for Drinking-water Quality provide a framework for protecting public health through management of drinking-water quality risks from catchment to consumer. The version published on 17 June 2026 incorporates the first, second and third addenda. It updates earlier editions to reflect new evidence and implementation experience.

## Risk management rather than one-number decisions
Water safety needs to be considered through contextual hazard identification, health-based targets, water safety plans and independent surveillance. A single measurement or a machine-learning classification cannot establish that a sample is safe. Assessment depends on relevant hazards, the local supply context, proper sampling, suitable laboratory methods and the applicable national requirements.

## Types of water-quality concerns
Drinking-water assessments may consider microbial hazards, chemical hazards, radiological aspects, and acceptability aspects such as taste, odour and appearance. Different hazards require suitable measurement methods and controls. A data set containing only physicochemical measurements cannot be assumed to assess every microbial or radiological hazard.

## Sampling, monitoring and verification
Use appropriate sampling and analytical methods. Interpret results in context and arrange further investigation or laboratory testing where there is a potential health concern, incomplete information or uncertainty. Monitoring and surveillance are parts of a broader water-safety approach.
