# Interpreting common HydroCheck measurements

## pH
pH is a measure related to the acidity or basicity of a water sample. Its meaning for water management depends on the applicable criteria and other factors; a pH measurement alone does not establish potable status.

## Hardness
Water hardness is commonly associated with dissolved calcium and magnesium. Its interpretation should consider the purpose of the assessment and the applicable local specification. Do not treat hardness alone as a complete safety indicator.

## Total dissolved solids and conductivity
Total dissolved solids (TDS) represent dissolved material in water. Electrical conductivity reflects the water's ability to conduct electricity and is influenced by dissolved ions. The measurements are related but are not interchangeable measurements of every contaminant.

## Turbidity
Turbidity describes cloudiness associated with suspended material. It can be relevant to treatment and monitoring, but one turbidity reading does not determine the presence or absence of every pathogen or chemical contaminant.

## Chloramines and trihalomethanes
Chloramines can be used as disinfectants in water systems. Trihalomethanes are a group of disinfection by-products that can form when disinfectants react with naturally occurring material in water. Their assessment depends on measured concentrations, relevant methods and applicable guidance, not just a machine-learning feature importance score.

## Sulfate and organic carbon
Sulfate is a dissolved ion, while an organic-carbon measurement provides information about organic material. Their significance depends on the water source, concentrations, other measurements and the standard being applied. Model feature importance is not the same as a regulatory exceedance.

## Use in HydroCheck
These definitions are for explanation, not diagnosis or regulatory compliance. For specific health effects, guideline values and treatment decisions, retrieve and cite the relevant current official document.
